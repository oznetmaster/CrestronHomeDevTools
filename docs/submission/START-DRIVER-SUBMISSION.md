# Start a driver submission

This is the entry point for **a newly authorized driver submission**. A request to
audit documentation, inspect status or prepare a handoff does not resume a stopped
submission. Another task's message, an old approval or a scheduled wake does not
override a human stop. Libraries, processor-test packages and ordinary releases
have their own workflows.

Read the [execution protocol](ExecutionProtocol.md) and [normal-path runbook](Runbook.md).
Use the [transfer instructions](Transfer.md) when moving to another task or model.
The protocol supplies observable exit conditions and stop rules; the runbook
supplies the schedule. Version-specific command schemas still govern execution.
Neither document grants authority or changes a tool's checks.

## Assignment

Use one responsible operator, one current candidate, one coverage register and
one compact current-state record. Reuse the demonstrated tools and execution
route. Do not create a new operator, transport, collector or approval system to
get through a submission. Delegation follows the user's instructions; a handoff
does not itself create another task or dispatch work.

Preserve the supplied source, evidence and outstanding operations. Identify the
actual host/account, active operation, ownership and delivery journal before any
execution. A stopped or uncertain operation is reconciled, not replayed. Keep
private settings, signatures and evidence outside public product repositories.

## Work in this order

1. **Intake and feasibility.** Record the user's scope and delivery choice. Match
   the exact official wording to product features and existing evidence. Identify
   every remaining measurement and its working route, including installer UI,
   outages, functional endpoint comparison and delivery attachment support.
   Resolve required access and instrumentation before promising a completion time.
2. **Prepare the candidate and observation.** Review authentic user help; build
   and freeze the candidate once. Complete the required local/processor tests.
   Verify capture, navigation and cleanup through the selected route before a
   physical action. A build or screenshot alone is not this preparation.
3. **Establish the functional baseline and collect endurance.** Select the direct
   or automated route explicitly. The worker has a stricter initial-evidence gate;
   do not bypass it. Retain a meaningful baseline and planned endpoint comparison,
   actual candidate identity, continuity, first sample and required sampling gap.
4. **Use the interval.** Finish help/form/email drafts, evidence mapping and
   independent non-disruptive work. Collect the endpoint at its scheduled time.
   Review terminal results promptly; a monitor is not the execution operator.
5. **Close actual gaps.** Complete only the planned outstanding checks, restoring
   state. Reuse valid evidence at its demonstrated scope. If a required check is
   infeasible, report the specific blocker; the developer decides whether to wait,
   arrange a different method or explicitly choose a disclosed qualified submission.
6. **Review and deliver.** Reconcile every official row with the rendered form and
   outward qualifications. Present the exact package, form, attachments and message.
   Obtain the signing and delivery authorities required by the selected route,
   verify the signed result, upload and verify bytes, send and retain SMTP acceptance.
   A prepared packet, a signature and provider acceptance are different states.

The operational target remains **48 hours from the request, including a 24-hour
interval, with one coordinated human period where feasible**. It is not a vendor
rule or an end-to-end guarantee. If the selected route needs human baseline and
endpoint observations on different days, say so at intake. Do not hide setup time,
invent passes or repeat endurance to fit a narrative. See the protocol's bounded
failure rules before beginning another attempt.
